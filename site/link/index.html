Representation → perturbation

Does a good representation make a good perturbation model?

The two components share sources, and will share encoders. That makes the question testable at two levels: do the cell types that are hard to represent give perturbation conditions that are hard to predict, and do encoders that score high on one scale score high on the other.

Item level: Parse PBMC in both components

The Parse 10M PBMC atlas is an annotation panel (cell type is the label, donors held out) and a cytokine screen (context is donor and cell type). Every perturbation condition therefore has a context whose representation difficulty is already estimated. Five donors overlap.

Cell type level

Mean difficulty over the type's cells (x) and over the cytokine conditions in that type (y), with standard errors of the means.

Donor × cell type level

One point per context shared by both components.

The confound

Raw correlation is near zero at both levels, and the reason is visible in the table below. The abundant types, CD4 and CD8 memory and naive T cells, are the hardest to represent, because the probe confuses sibling states, and the easiest to predict perturbations for, because a condition with a thousand cells has a precise pseudobulk and the mean shift is easy to beat. Cells per condition pulls the two difficulties in opposite directions.

Controlling for cells per condition, the partial correlation turns positive but small. Sixteen types and sixty-nine contexts are too few to settle it; the screen-level test with shared encoders below is the one with power.

Model level: shared encoders

A perturbation respondent family in which the predictor is held fixed and only the encoder varies: cells are embedded with each representation model, the perturbation's latent shift is learned in that space, and the prediction is decoded back to genes. Every encoder then has an ability on both scales. Random projections at the same dimension are the null: if they track PCA in the perturbation component, dimension matters and representation quality does not.

Reading

Across the 27 classical encoders the two abilities move together: Spearman +0.94 on the unseen-combination scale and +0.95 on the unseen-perturbation scale, and still +0.95 with the random projections removed. It is not only dimension. At equal dimension the random-projection null sits far below PCA in both components (128D: −0.07 and −1.27 against +0.69 and +0.63 for PCA), and ICA-64D lands exactly where PCA-64D does, as a rotation of the same subspace should.

The mapping breaks inside the top cluster. Nyström kernel features are the best annotation encoder (+0.72) but a poor response basis (−0.57, against +0.87 for PCA-256D): the nonlinear features that separate cell types do not decode perturbation responses through a linear read-out. And the whole family sits two to five logits below the models that learn their basis from responses (PCA regression +5.8, MLP +4.9, low-rank +4.5 on the same scale). A subspace fitted to cells carries perturbation responses much worse than a subspace fitted to responses; representation quality orders the encoders within a family, it does not close that gap.

Foundation-model encoders enter this table through the same recipe once their embeddings are on the representation scale; see the contribute page.

Type level: does it transfer within a cell type?

The sharper question: if an encoder annotates cell type X well, does it predict perturbations in X well? On Parse PBMC every encoder gets one representation ability per type (the cells of X) and one perturbation ability per type (the conditions whose context is X), anchored to the panel-level and screen-level item difficulties. Subtracting the encoder's overall ability on each side leaves the type-specific part, which is what would have to transfer.
